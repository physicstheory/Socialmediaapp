What makes Ember different

Safety is built into how Ember works, not hidden in a settings menu.

👥

Friends first

The app opens on the Following feed: videos posted by friends your child has accepted. Discover is a separate tab, and you can switch it off.

💬

No strangers in DMs

Only accepted friends can send messages. Adults can't search for, view or message anyone under 18.

🛡️

Automatic filtering

Every video, comment and message is checked. Dangerous challenges, self-harm promotion, sexual content and bullying are blocked outright.

🔒

Parent PIN

Lock the settings with a PIN so filters, privacy and friend approvals can't be changed without you.

🚫

Grooming red flags blocked

In chats that include a young person, messages asking for secrets, photos, addresses, ages or meetups are stopped and logged.

📍

Private by default

Accounts for under-16s are always private. Phone numbers, emails, addresses and links are hidden from teens' chats, and photos are stripped of location data.

Set it up together (5 minutes)

Doing this side by side is the best way to start, and it's a good time to talk about online safety.

  1. Open Ember and add it to the home screen. On iPhone, tap Share then Add to Home Screen. On Android, tap ⋮ then Install app.
  2. Create the account with your child's real birthday. That's what switches on the right protections, and the birthday is never shown to anyone.
  3. Choose interests and filters together. Pick topics they love, then review the filters. Anything locked for their age shows a 🔒.
  4. Set a parent PIN. This is required for under-13s and optional for teens. Keep it to yourself: it's needed to change settings and, for kids, to add friends.
  5. Add yourself as a friend. Share your own friend code (Friends → My friend code) and have your child enter it. Adults can only connect with under-18s this way.

What Ember can't promise

We'd rather be honest than overpromise:
  • Ember's automatic filters read text: captions, hashtags, titles and messages. They can't fully judge what's inside a video or photo, so reporting and parental involvement still matter.
  • Videos from TikTok and YouTube play in those companies' official players. Ember filters what it shows, but tapping through to TikTok or YouTube takes your child outside Ember's protections.
  • Age protections depend on the birthday entered. Please make sure it's correct.
  • No app replaces talking with your child. Ember's report and block buttons are on every video, profile and message. Show your child where they are.

Questions parents ask

Can I read my child's messages?

Not from your own account. Messages stay private between friends, which helps kids trust the app and come to you when something's wrong. What you control is who they can talk to (friends only, no adults unless you approve them by friend code) and what gets blocked automatically.

Do messages really disappear?

Chats can disappear 24 hours after they're read, like Snapchat, and photo Snaps can be viewed once. Anything that's reported is kept as evidence, and messages blocked by the safety filter are logged.

What happens when something is reported?

The video is hidden from the person who reported it straight away, and it's hidden for everyone after several reports. Accounts that get reported repeatedly are blocked from sending friend requests. Reported messages and Snaps are kept so they can be reviewed.

Does Ember show ads or sell data?

No. There are no ads and no data sales. Birthdays are only used to set protections and are never shown to other users.

How do I delete my child's account?

Go to Settings → Account → Delete account. For kids' accounts, the parent PIN is required.
